# Search Opportunity Guidance

## Guiding Principle

Top search volume shows demand. Search gaps show opportunity. AuditMap should build for both.

Official and municipal sources will often remain the default result for broad, high-authority queries. AuditMap should answer those useful questions clearly, but it should not assume that repeating official information will create a distinct reason to visit or trust an AuditMap page.

AuditMap's advantage is not bureaucracy. Its advantage is perception: practical synthesis, lived experience, emotional clarity, timely observation, beauty, and locally attentive judgment.

## Dual-Track Standard

Every place audit and publication should do two things at the same time:

1. Answer the main high-demand questions people already ask.
2. Identify at least one meaningful search gap AuditMap can serve unusually well.

A search gap is a useful visitor question, angle, or need that official sources answer poorly, fragment across several pages, or do not address at all. It must be specific enough to guide research or community contribution. `More information about this park` is not a search gap.

## Required Search Opportunity Brief

Every audited place should carry one reviewable search opportunity brief with:

- **Gap question:** The concrete question a visitor might ask.
- **Distinct angle:** What AuditMap can add beyond a restatement of official facts.
- **Visitor value:** The decision, anxiety, or experience this helps someone understand.
- **Official coverage gap:** What appears missing, fragmented, overly formal, or unclear in consulted sources.
- **Evidence plan:** What field observation, community confirmation, comparison, photograph, or authoritative source is needed before publication.
- **Opportunity type:** One of `feeling`, `audience-fit`, `free-things`, `overlooked-detail`, `access-comfort`, `time-season`, `comparison`, `timely-observation`, or `story`.
- **Confidence:** Internal confidence in the opportunity hypothesis, not confidence that an unsupported claim is true.

The brief is planning material. It does not make a lived-experience claim publishable by itself.

## UX Implications

Every listing should help visitors find both the obvious information they came for and the deeper insight they may not have known to ask for. Product surfaces should make room for:

- real visitor questions
- practical on-the-ground insight
- comparisons and recommendations
- feeling, atmosphere, and story
- timely observations and changes

Utility comes first, but it should lead naturally into meaning. The page should help someone understand a place, not only retrieve facts about it.

## Brand Implications

AuditMap is a trusted, observant, human layer on top of the map, not a copy of an official listing.

The voice should be:

- grounded
- clear
- generous
- emotionally intelligent
- locally attentive
- poetic when appropriate, but never vague

Official facts should remain precise and sourced. Human interpretation should remain honest about who observed it, when it was observed, and where reasonable experiences may differ.

## Content Opportunities

Look especially for useful gaps around:

- what a place feels like
- who it is genuinely good for
- free things to do
- overlooked details
- access, ease, shade, seating, noise, and comfort
- seasonal and time-of-day differences
- emotionally honest recommendations
- comparisons that help someone choose between nearby public places

## Trust Guardrails

- Do not claim an opportunity is `low competition`, `high volume`, or likely to rank without evidence.
- Do not turn one review or one visit into a universal statement.
- Do not present inference, atmosphere, or audience fit as an official fact.
- Attribute time-sensitive and lived observations and show their dates.
- Use community notes and field work to test a gap before making strong editorial claims.
- Preserve disagreement when experiences reasonably differ.
- Never weaken the core factual record to make room for a more distinctive angle.

## Publication Gate

Before a place is considered fully enriched, confirm:

- the highest-interest visitor questions have clear, sourced answers
- one search opportunity brief has been identified
- the opportunity adds value beyond the official source
- unsupported parts have an explicit evidence plan
- the page language distinguishes verified fact, community observation, and editorial synthesis

## Working Rule

For every place AuditMap audits or publishes, identify at least one gap AuditMap can uniquely and responsibly serve so the page has a genuine chance to earn attention, clicks, and trust.
