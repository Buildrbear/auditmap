# AuditMap Nationwide Major Parks Launch List

## Purpose

This is a decision list, not a publishing claim. It identifies the first major urban parks AuditMap should evaluate for nationwide page production. Small neighborhood parks, municipal buildings, libraries, ordinary recreation centers, and other civic facilities are intentionally excluded from this launch.

The city-market baseline follows the national frame used by Trust for Public Land's ParkScore work: the 100 largest U.S. cities, supplemented by a small number of distinct regional or tourism markets such as Cary, Providence, Charleston, Savannah, and Honolulu.

## Selection Rule

A launch candidate should satisfy at least one of these:

- It is the city's widely recognized signature park.
- It is a large destination with multiple internal areas that merit subsites.
- It is a major waterfront, river, trail, cultural, or family destination used by visitors across the city or region.
- It generates recurring high-intent questions about parking, entrances, restrooms, accessibility, closures, transit, play, water, trails, landmarks, or events.

Being famous is not sufficient by itself. The park must support a useful, detailed AuditMap page.

## Production Gate For Every Approved Park

- At least three real, source-cited photographs; no AI-generated images.
- One canonical park page with visible answers in raw HTML.
- Essential facts: address, hours, cost, parking, entrances, restrooms, accessibility, transit, closures, safety-sensitive arrival notes, and official contacts.
- Major internal destinations modeled as subsites when the park is large or complex.
- Each subsite needs its own image, location guidance, description, and source.
- A freshness schedule and unresolved-question queue.
- Official sources first; reviewed community evidence can fill documented gaps.

## Launch Tiers

- **Anchor:** Produce first. Usually the one park that best represents the city.
- **Supporting:** Produce after the anchor. Include only when it adds a meaningfully different daily-use or visitor experience.
- **Hold:** Do not produce yet. Keep out of the launch until its significance or page depth is confirmed.

## Review Notes

- Park ownership can be municipal, county, state, federal, or a public conservancy. Public access and destination importance matter more than the operator.
- Combined systems such as Minneapolis Chain of Lakes or Richmond's James River Park should generally be one parent page with subsites, not many competing canonical pages.
- Large linear parks and waterfront systems should use subsites for major access points and destinations.
- Candidate names and operating authorities must be verified against current official sources before page production.
- This list should remain deliberately small. Adding every park now would weaken research quality and delay the nationwide launch.

The machine-readable candidate inventory is in `data/nationwide-major-parks-launch.csv`.
