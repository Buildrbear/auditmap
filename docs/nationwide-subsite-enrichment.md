# Nationwide Park Subsite Enrichment

## Objective

Give every major-park page a useful internal map of named destinations, with a
real sourced photograph for every park and every published subsite.

## What Counts As A Subsite

A subsite must be a named or clearly mapped destination that a visitor may
reasonably need to find. Publish:

- entrances, parking areas, and trailheads
- playgrounds, splash pads, pools, and water-play areas
- restrooms and visitor or information centers
- dog areas
- named trails and major trail junction destinations
- fields, courts, sports complexes, and recreation centers
- gardens, lakes, beaches, overlooks, and nature areas
- public art, monuments, museums, and landmarks inside the park
- event lawns, amphitheaters, pavilions, and gathering spaces

Do not publish ordinary benches, unnamed paths, minor utility structures,
individual parking aisles, or every field in a repeated field complex.

## Publication Gate

Every published subsite must have:

- a unique stable ID and slug within its parent park
- a useful name and normalized feature type
- coordinates inside the parent park or a documented boundary exception
- a position-quality label
- a concise visitor-focused description
- an authoritative source URL and verification date
- at least one real photograph
- image source, author or owner, license label, and alt text
- clear answers to the main visitor questions for that destination
- one reviewable search opportunity brief that identifies a useful gap beyond the official record

The search opportunity brief must include a gap question, distinct angle,
visitor value, official coverage gap, evidence plan, opportunity type, and
internal confidence. It remains planning material until the underlying
observation or claim has appropriate evidence. See
[Search Opportunity Guidance](search-opportunity-guidance.md).

The preferred range is 3-12 subsites per park. Larger destination parks may
exceed 12 when their internal areas are independently useful. Small signature
parks may publish fewer when no additional destinations can be verified.

## Source Priority

1. Official park maps and municipal or operating-authority pages
2. Official GIS/open-data records
3. OpenStreetMap mapped features inside a verified park boundary
4. Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons
5. Reviewed community evidence

OpenStreetMap discoveries are candidates until they pass duplicate, coordinate,
description, and photo review.

## Image Priority

1. Wikimedia Commons images that explicitly identify the subsite
2. Geotagged Commons images visually confirmed to depict the subsite
3. Official park or municipal page images with the source retained
4. Reviewed community photographs

Nearby imagery without a clear visual or textual match is not publishable.

## Rollout

1. Ten-park pilot across different park types and regions
2. Remaining anchor parks in regional batches
3. Supporting parks in regional batches
4. Nationwide completeness, duplication, and visual-quality audit
