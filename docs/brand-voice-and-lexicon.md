## Mission

### Canonical mission statement

## The Public's Guide to Public Places

Public places deserve more than a pin on a map.

AuditMap brings together verified information, community notes, photography, video, and 360° tours to give public spaces the attention they deserve.

Together, we can find the neighborhood park that feels just right. We can finally join the pickup soccer game we always pass but never have time to stop for. We can discover the place with something for everyone, from the dog and the kids to the grandparents.

Every photo, note, correction, and contribution makes the next visit easier. Every place we document becomes more visible, useful, and welcoming.

**Browse. Explore. Leave a note.**

Use this statement unchanged for the primary About or mission surface. Shorter product surfaces may use excerpts, but should preserve its meaning and vocabulary.

## What A Note Means

`Note` is AuditMap's inclusive word for a small act that improves the public guide. A note can be:

- a practical tip
- a question or answer
- a current photo or video
- a confirmation
- a correction
- an accessibility observation
- a field check
- support that funds the next piece of public work

Use `leave a note` when inviting broad participation. Use the specific action when a person has already chosen a task, such as `add a photo`, `confirm this detail`, or `back this upgrade`.

## Voice

AuditMap should sound welcoming, curious, practical, and quietly optimistic. It should feel like an invitation from another explorer, not a corporate campaign or government form.

AuditMap is a trusted, observant, human layer on top of the map. It should not sound like a rewritten municipal page. Our advantage is perception: grounded synthesis, practical truth, emotional clarity, and locally attentive observation.

The voice should be grounded, clear, generous, emotionally intelligent, and poetic when appropriate without becoming vague. Official facts remain precise and sourced. Feeling, atmosphere, audience fit, and recommendations should be framed as dated observation or transparent synthesis rather than universal fact.

Prefer:

- `Help the next visitor`
- `Share what you found`
- `Built by explorers. Useful to everyone.`
- `Find a place that feels right`
- `What we build stays free for everyone`
- `Help give this public place the attention it deserves`

Avoid:

- guilt or pressure
- fake urgency or unsupported impact numbers
- donor-first language that makes money the only valuable contribution
- claims that support can influence factual conclusions
- corporate growth language such as `users`, `acquisition`, or `monetization` in public-facing copy
- framing public places as products to consume

## Message Hierarchy

1. Help someone find and enjoy a public place.
2. Show the practical information AuditMap provides.
3. Add one useful insight the official listing does not provide well.
4. Invite them to leave one useful note.
5. Explain that many small contributions build a richer public guide.
6. Offer funding as one of several ways to participate.

## Search Opportunity Voice

Top search volume shows demand. Search gaps show opportunity. AuditMap should answer common questions while also helping visitors understand what a place feels like, who it serves well, what is easy or difficult, what changes with season or time, and which overlooked details matter.

Do not claim that AuditMap will outrank an official source merely because it answers a popular question. Do not manufacture novelty. A distinct angle must remain useful, supportable, and consistent with the trust guardrails in [Search Opportunity Guidance](search-opportunity-guidance.md).
