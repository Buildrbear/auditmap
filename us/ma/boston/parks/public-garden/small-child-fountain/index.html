What people say

The Small Child Fountain is in the southwest garden near Arlington and Boylston Streets. It is an ornamental sculpture and fountain, not a splash pad or drinking-water feature. Swan Boats are seasonal, weather dependent, and can pause for high winds or storms. Flower displays, bridge access, and event routes also change through the year.

Swan Boats and Lagoon

The Swan Boat dock is on the east side of the lagoon near Charles Street. Rides are first-come, seasonal, weather permitting, and usually last about 10 to 15 minutes.

Ether Monument

The Ether Monument is on the garden's northwest side near Arlington and Beacon Streets. It is reached from the main paths and offers benches and shade, but it is not an entrance or staffed visitor facility.

Where should I park for Small Child Fountain? +

There is no public lot in the garden. Use transit, nearby paid garages, or legal metered parking; Boston Common Garage is across Charles Street.

Are there restrooms near Small Child Fountain? +

There is no dependable general-purpose restroom inside the Public Garden. Use confirmed-open facilities on Boston Common or nearby businesses before settling in.

Is Small Child Fountain free? +

Garden access and monuments are free. Swan Boats charge per ride; parking, food, and special programs cost separately.

How accessible is Small Child Fountain? +

Main garden paths and lagoon approaches are generally accessible, but historic surfaces, bridge grades, crowds, snow, ice, and temporary work can affect routes.

Are dogs allowed at Small Child Fountain? +

Pets are not allowed in the Public Garden except qualifying service animals. Do not use Boston Common dog rules for this separate park.

Is Small Child Fountain good for children? +

Duckling statues and Swan Boats are the family anchors. The lawns and flower beds are protected landscapes, so use paths and benches rather than treating the garden as a play field.
