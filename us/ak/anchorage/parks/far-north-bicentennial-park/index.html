What people say

Far North Bicentennial Park is Anchorage's largest municipal park, spanning about 4,011 acres with more than 80 miles of year-round trails for walking, running, hiking, skiing, mountain biking, skijoring, dog mushing, and other designated uses. It connects Hillside Park, Campbell Creek routes, BLM Campbell Tract, and one of the country's largest contiguous urban-wildland systems.

Where should I enter Far North Bicentennial Park? +

Choose a named trailhead rather than navigating to the park center. The Service and Abbott Road area at 5577 Abbott Road serves the newer Hillside singletrack system; other entrances serve Campbell Creek, BLM Campbell Tract, ski, mushing, and multi-use routes.

How many trails are in Far North Bicentennial Park? +

The 4,011-acre park supports more than 80 miles of trails. Surfaces and designated uses vary widely, including paved or groomed multi-use corridors, earthen singletrack, ski tracks, dog-mushing routes, and natural hiking paths.

Where should I park for Far North Bicentennial Park? +

Park at the mapped trailhead that serves your route, such as Abbott Road, Service High School/Hillside, or a Campbell Tract access. Lots are separated by a very large trail network and may not connect conveniently to the same loop.

Does Far North Bicentennial Park connect to city trails? +

Yes. Routes connect with the Campbell Creek greenbelt and adjacent BLM Campbell Tract, forming a major non-motorized corridor through Anchorage's urban wildland. Use current wayfinding and trail maps for junctions.

Are there restrooms in Far North Bicentennial Park? +

Do not assume restrooms or drinking fountains are available along a route. Municipal trail guidance says drinking fountains are few and creek water is unsafe; use facilities at a developed trailhead when provided and carry water.

Can I bring a dog to Far North Bicentennial Park? +

Dogs use many Anchorage trails, but leash, skijoring, mushing, groomed-track, and wildlife rules vary by route and season. Keep full control, protect set ski tracks, remove waste, and use the designated trail map.

Are Far North Bicentennial Park trails accessible? +

Accessibility varies sharply by trailhead and route. Some multi-use corridors and new bridges are wider and developed, while singletrack and natural routes include roots, grades, mud, snow, and narrow sections. Confirm the exact route before relying on it.

What should I check before using Far North Bicentennial Park trails? +

Check grooming, construction, freeze-thaw and mud conditions, daylight, and wildlife notices. Anchorage specifically warns that trails are shared with bears, moose, and coyotes and recommends making noise in bear country.

Where can dogs be off leash in Far North Bicentennial Park? +

The North Gasline Trail is Anchorage's designated off-leash trail in this park. Dogs must be leashed while entering and leaving, licensed and vaccinated, under control and in sight, and owners must remove waste; other trails may require a leash or have seasonal restrictions.

Which Far North Bicentennial trails are reserved for dog mushing? +

Anchorage's dog-mushing network includes dedicated routes maintained by the Alaska Sled Dog and Racing Association. Do not walk, bike, ski, or bring loose pets onto a posted mushing-only trail, because doing so can endanger visitors, dogs, and mushers.

Can I use Far North Bicentennial Park after dark? +

Treat posted trailhead and park hours as controlling, and plan around Anchorage's rapidly changing seasonal daylight. Bring illumination for any permitted low-light outing, but do not assume every gate, lot, or groomed route remains available overnight.

Where can visitors warm up or get out of the weather near Far North Bicentennial Park? +

The boreal forest provides summer shade but is not protection from wind, lightning, extreme cold, or falling trees. The adjacent BLM Campbell Creek Science Center offers indoor exhibits and restrooms Monday through Friday, 9 a.m.-4 p.m., except federal holidays, with its gate generally open 8 a.m.-5 p.m.; it is not an after-hours emergency shelter. Carry weather layers and return to a vehicle or open substantial building before conditions deteriorate.

Is there a playground at Far North Bicentennial Park? +

Anchorage does not identify a conventional playground within Far North Bicentennial Park. This is a 4,000-acre urban wildland with more than 80 miles of trails for hiking, biking, skiing, dog mushing, skijoring, and wildlife viewing. For play equipment, choose one of Anchorage's neighborhood or inclusive playgrounds instead. Here, select a short mapped route, carry layers and bear-aware supplies, and keep children close around wildlife, creek crossings, winter cold, bikes, and remote trail junctions.

Where can I picnic at Far North Bicentennial Park? +

The clearest developed picnic option is the shelter at Abbott Loop Community Park trailhead on Elmore Road near 80th Avenue, which also provides direct access to the Far North trail network. Other major access points such as Smokejumper, Campbell Airstrip, and the Bivouac trailheads are primarily parking and trail access, not guaranteed picnic facilities. Anchorage publishes a general park and shelter rental process but does not clearly list this shelter's reservability online, so contact Parks before planning exclusive use; for a trail meal, pack out all food and waste and follow bear-aware practices.

Can children swim or use a splash pad at Far North Bicentennial Park? +

No developed splash pad, pool, or designated swim beach is listed in Far North Bicentennial Park. Its creeks, wetlands, and small waters are natural habitat, not supervised water-play facilities; avoid entering them and expect cold water, unstable banks, wildlife, and seasonal ice. Goose Lake Park is Anchorage's nearby developed lake-and-beach destination, but its current swimming and lifeguard status should be confirmed before visiting.

What are the main landmarks in Far North Bicentennial Park? +

This is an urban-wilderness trail system rather than a monument or public-art park. Use the Campbell Creek Science Center area, Campbell Airstrip Road trail access, or a named trailhead as your orientation point. Campbell Creek, bridge crossings, wetlands, and the dedicated winter mushing-trail network are defining features. Never enter groomed dog-sled trails on foot, skis, or bicycle when snow-covered, because fast teams can approach quietly.

How much does Far North Bicentennial Park cost? +

General park and trail access are free. Programs, permits, rentals, and organized events may charge.
