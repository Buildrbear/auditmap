Mission Bay Park

Mission Bay Park, San Diego, CA
Hours ⌄
Published schedule Mission Bay destinations commonly allow use from 4:00 a.m. to 2:00 a.m., but gates, parking lots, Fiesta Island, beaches, launches, lifeguards, and facilities use location-specific hours. Check the exact cove or island.

What people say

Mission Bay Park is a 4,235-acre aquatic park with 27 miles of shoreline, 19 miles of sandy beaches, eight designated swimming areas, nearly 14 miles of bicycle paths, playgrounds, picnic and fire-ring areas, public restrooms and showers, boat docks and launches, watercraft rentals, sports courts, wildlife preserves, Fiesta Island, and numerous named coves and islands.

Where should I go in Mission Bay Park? +

Choose a named destination before navigating: Bonita Cove, Crown Point, De Anza Cove, Fiesta Island, Hospitality Point, Mariner's Point, Mission Point, Ski Beach, Tecolote Shores, Vacation Isle, or another mapped site. The general park pin covers more than 4,200 acres.

City of San Diego ↗
Is parking free at Mission Bay Park? +

Most public lots are free, but gates and overnight restrictions vary by location. Mission Point's lots are free and close at 10 p.m.; Fiesta Island allows parking around the island during its 4 a.m.-10 p.m. hours.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026

Are there restrooms at Mission Bay Park? +

Public restrooms and showers are available at many developed beach, cove, playground, and launch areas, but not everywhere. Fiesta Island has no public restrooms, while Mission Point and Model Yacht Pond have facilities.

What are the paths and surfaces like at Mission Bay Park? +

Paved approaches end at sand, shoreline, or launch surfaces. Choose the mapped paved promenade or accessibility point when firm continuous travel matters.

Are there playgrounds at Mission Bay Park? +

Yes. Children's playgrounds are distributed across the bay; Mission Point specifically has a tot lot beside picnic tables, fire rings, paths, showers, and restrooms. Use the facility map to find the closest family area.

Where can dogs go at Mission Bay Park? +

Fiesta Island is leash-optional except in fenced nesting and youth-camp areas. Elsewhere in Mission Bay, leashed dogs are allowed only outside seasonal daytime restrictions: before 9 a.m. and after 4 p.m. in winter or after 6 p.m. in summer.

Casual picnicking is generally practical in designated lawn or table areas. Reserved shelters, permitted events, and sports use can control a specific space.

How much shade is available at Mission Bay Park? +

The beach and shoreline are exposed. Bring your own sun protection; shade is more likely near developed picnic structures than on the sand.

Where can visitors swim at Mission Bay Park? +

Mission Bay has 19 miles of sandy beach and eight officially designated swimming areas, but it is a large aquatic park with separate boating, speed, and special-use zones rather than one continuous swim beach. Choose a named bay destination and swim only inside posted swimming areas; vessels may not use designated swim zones. Lifeguard coverage and restrooms vary by location and season, and visitors should check San Diego County's current beach-and-bay closure and water-quality status before entering.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026
City of San Diego ↗
How do I reach Mission Bay Park by transit? +

Use MTS Route 8 or 9 from Old Town for the western and southern bay, Route 30 for Pacific Beach and the northern edge, or a Blue Line trolley station for an east-bay connection. Mission Bay covers more than 4,200 acres, so enter a named destination such as Bonita Cove, Crown Point, De Anza Cove, Fiesta Island, or Tecolote Shores in the live planner; no single stop is convenient to the entire park and the final walk can be long.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026
City of San Diego ↗
Which part of Mission Bay Park should I visit? +

Mission Bay has no single central landmark or entrance. Bonita Cove is a strong family choice for a playground, guarded water access, fire rings, picnic tables, and restrooms; Crown Point and Tecolote Shores provide broad shoreline parks; Fiesta Island is a large event, cycling, dog, and open-space destination; De Anza Cove, Ski Beach, and Vacation Isle serve other parts of the bay. Route to the named cove or island, not only to Mission Bay Park.

General park, beach, path, playground, and most public parking access are free. Permits, rentals, launches, moorings, concessions, attractions, and organized events may charge.
