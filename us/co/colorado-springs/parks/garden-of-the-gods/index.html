What people say

Garden of the Gods is a free Colorado Springs city park and National Natural Landmark centered on dramatic red-rock formations. Visitors can use scenic roads, multiple parking areas, picnic sites, the paved Perkins Central Garden Trail, natural-surface hiking and biking routes, seasonal shuttle service, and the separate Visitor & Nature Center.

Where should I start at Garden of the Gods? +

First-time visitors can start at the Visitor & Nature Center off North 30th Street for maps, exhibits, staff guidance, restrooms, and an overview before crossing into the park. If you already know your trail, navigate to its numbered parking area rather than only to the park name.

Where should I park at Garden of the Gods? +

The park has numbered lots near major formations and trailheads, plus the Visitor Center lot and a naturally surfaced overflow lot. Parking is limited on busy summer days; arrive early or use the complimentary seasonal shuttle when it operates. Lots 1 and 16 are the designated choices for larger vehicles and trailers.

What hours is Garden of the Gods open? +

Park gates are open 5:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. November 1-April 30 and 5:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. May 1-October 31. The Visitor & Nature Center is generally open 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. in winter and until 6:00 p.m. in summer, with holiday closures.

Is Garden of the Gods free? +

Yes. Entry to the city park and Visitor & Nature Center is free. Optional guided tours, films, purchases, programs, and commercial activities can have separate prices.

What is the most accessible way to see Garden of the Gods? +

Begin at the wheelchair-accessible Visitor & Nature Center, then use accessible parking near the paved, low-grade Perkins Central Garden Trail. Accessible parking appears on the official map; natural-surface trails and some viewpoints have uneven grades. The seasonal shuttle helps with distance but confirm current vehicle accessibility before relying on it.

Where are restrooms at Garden of the Gods? +

The Visitor & Nature Center has accessible restrooms. Additional restrooms are marked near selected park parking and picnic areas on the official map, while some are summer-only; use the center before a long trail outing when availability matters.

Is there an easy paved trail at Garden of the Gods? +

Yes. The Perkins Central Garden Trail is the principal paved, relatively low-grade route among the central formations. The park also has longer natural-surface trails, including a roughly 2.5-mile easy route from the naturally surfaced overflow lot.

What can limit access at Garden of the Gods? +

Parking congestion, winter weather, wildfire conditions, construction, permitted events, and seasonal shuttle or restroom schedules can change a visit. The east face of North Gateway Rock currently has a climbing closure, and all technical climbing requires a permit and proper gear.

Are dogs allowed at Garden of the Gods? +

Yes. Pets must remain on a leash no longer than six feet and stay on trail, and owners must remove waste. A designated off-leash area lies east of Rock Ledge Ranch and south of Gateway Road; dogs must still remain under control there.

Where can I picnic at Garden of the Gods? +

Use the designated Scotsman or South Spring Canyon picnic areas. Receptions are not allowed elsewhere in the park, overnight stays are prohibited, and seasonal fire restrictions can limit cooking or open flame.

What are the main sights at Garden of the Gods? +

Major landmarks include North and South Gateway Rocks, Balanced Rock, Siamese Twins, Central Garden, High Point, and views of Pikes Peak. Start with the free Visitor and Nature Center for maps because parking and trail access differ by formation.

City of Colorado Springs ↗
Where can visitors find shade or weather shelter at Garden of the Gods? +

Natural shade is limited and uneven among the exposed red-rock formations, paved Central Garden, overlooks, and many trails. Scotsman and South Spring Canyon picnic areas have scattered trees, but visitors should carry sun protection and water. The accessible Visitor and Nature Center provides indoor exhibits, cafe, water refill stations, and restrooms during posted hours, generally 9 a.m.-6 p.m. in summer. Rock overhangs and trees are not lightning shelters; return to the center or a vehicle before storms.

Is there a playground at Garden of the Gods? +

Garden of the Gods does not advertise a conventional playground. It is a protected National Natural Landmark focused on red-rock scenery, paved and natural trails, geology, wildlife, guided programs, and the Visitor and Nature Center's exhibits. For equipment play, choose a Colorado Springs neighborhood park rather than climbing on the formations, which is prohibited outside permitted technical climbing. For a child-friendly visit, start at the center, use the easy Central Garden route, carry water, and keep children on marked trails and away from roads and drop-offs.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026
Garden of the Gods Visitor and Nature Center ↗
Is there a splash pad, pool, or lazy river at Garden of the Gods? +

No. Garden of the Gods has no public pool, splash pad, or lazy river; it is a protected dry-land natural landmark for trails, geology, climbing under permit rules, and scenic touring. A City post dated April 1, 2026 announcing a lazy river was an April Fools joke, not a real project. The completed waterline supplies fire protection and future restroom capacity, not recreational water play. Use a separately listed Colorado Springs pool or sprayground instead.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026
City of Colorado Springs ↗
Can I reach Garden of the Gods by city bus? +

Mountain Metro Route 3 travels along Colorado Avenue toward Manitou Springs and provides the most useful fixed-route approach, but it does not carry visitors around the park. Plan a final walk or rideshare to the Visitor and Nature Center or selected trailhead. Do not confuse Route 34 on Garden of the Gods Road with direct service to the rock formations, and verify any seasonal internal shuttle separately.
