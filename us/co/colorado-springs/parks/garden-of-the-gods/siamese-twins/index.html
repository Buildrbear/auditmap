What people say

Siamese Twins is a paired sandstone formation reached by the natural-surface Siamese Twins Trail, with a framed view toward Pikes Peak through its window. This is not the paved Central Garden route. Expect uneven natural surfaces, sun, wind, and limited shade; stay on the official trail, carry water, avoid sandstone after wet weather, and do not climb without the required permit and safety equipment.

Where should I park for Siamese Twins? +

The park has numbered lots near major formations and trailheads, plus the Visitor Center lot and a naturally surfaced overflow lot. Parking is limited on busy summer days; arrive early or use the complimentary seasonal shuttle when it operates. Lots 1 and 16 are the designated choices for larger vehicles and trailers. Are there restrooms near Siamese Twins? +

The Visitor & Nature Center has accessible restrooms. Additional restrooms are marked near selected park parking and picnic areas on the official map, while some are summer-only; use the center before a long trail outing when availability matters.

How accessible is Siamese Twins? +

Begin at the wheelchair-accessible Visitor & Nature Center, then use accessible parking near the paved, low-grade Perkins Central Garden Trail. Accessible parking appears on the official map; natural-surface trails and some viewpoints have uneven grades. The seasonal shuttle helps with distance but confirm current vehicle accessibility before relying on it.

Are dogs allowed at Siamese Twins? +

Yes. Pets must remain on a leash no longer than six feet and stay on trail, and owners must remove waste. A designated off-leash area lies east of Rock Ledge Ranch and south of Gateway Road; dogs must still remain under control there. Separately operated buildings, beaches, gardens, and attractions can set stricter rules.

Is Siamese Twins useful for a family visit? +

Siamese Twins is a paired sandstone formation reached by the natural-surface Siamese Twins Trail, with a framed view toward Pikes Peak through its window. Garden of the Gods does not advertise a conventional playground. It is a protected National Natural Landmark focused on red-rock scenery, paved and natural trails, geology, wildlife, guided programs, and the Visitor and Nature Center's exhibits. For equipment play, choose a Colorado Springs neighborhood park rather than climbing on the formations, which is prohibited outside permitted technical climbing. For a child-friendly visit, start at the center, use the easy Central Garden route, carry water, and keep children on marked trails and away from roads and drop-offs.
