What people say

Lassiter Mill Historical Park is a small hidden-gem stop on Crabtree Creek with a dam, surviving mill remnants, an interpretive plaque, fishing and picnic space, and access to the paved 18-mile Crabtree Creek Greenway corridor.

Where should I park for Lassiter Mill Park? +

Use the City-listed greenway lot at 3300 Lassiter Mill Road and the park approach at Lassiter Falls Circle. Parking is limited, so arrive early and use only marked spaces; nearby residential, business, and no-parking areas are not overflow. Construction staging beginning in 2026 may change the practical entrance or available spaces.

Which entrance should I use for Lassiter Mill Park? +

Use 3300 Lassiter Falls Circle for the historic dam, mill-remnant overlook, plaque, lawn, and picnic area. The City also identifies 3300 Lassiter Mill Road as the greenway parking point. Trail construction can change crossings near the road and bridge, so follow current signs rather than an older saved route.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Are there restrooms at Lassiter Mill Park? +

Do not plan on a public restroom at this small historic and greenway access site. Raleigh's official parking and project pages do not list one, and there is no community center. Use a restroom before arriving, especially before a longer Crabtree Creek Trail ride or walk.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
What time does Lassiter Mill Park close? +

Use the park and Crabtree Creek Greenway from dawn to dusk under Raleigh's general operating rule. The site has no staffed visitor center or lit recreation facility, and the dam and creek bank become harder to navigate safely in low light.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026

Is Lassiter Mill Park free? +

Yes. General entry, the historic overlook, picnic area, and greenway access are free. Fishing requires the appropriate current North Carolina license and compliance with posted rules, but the City does not list a separate park admission or parking fee.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Is Lassiter Mill Park wheelchair and stroller accessible? +

The parking approach and paved Crabtree Creek Trail are the most predictable routes. The current corridor also includes an unpaved section, steps, and changing bank conditions near Lassiter Mill Road, while the dam overlook has grass, rocks, and uneven edges. Construction is intended to improve this connection but will temporarily change access.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Can I access Crabtree Creek Greenway at Lassiter Mill? +

Yes. Lassiter Mill is a City-listed parking and access point on the paved 18-mile Crabtree Creek corridor, which connects west toward Kiwanis Park, Crabtree Valley and Umstead and east toward Anderson Point and the Neuse River Trail. The local road crossing and unpaved segment are entering a major improvement project.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Will construction affect Lassiter Mill Park and the greenway? +

Yes. Raleigh plans construction from August 2026 through fall 2028 on the Crabtree Creek corridor between Hartford Street and Lassiter Mill Road, including the unpaved segment, stream bank, boardwalk and steps, and the road bridge area. Expect signed detours, closures, or altered parking and check the project and greenway alerts before visiting.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
What remains of the historic Lassiter Mill? +

Visitors can see the dam, remnants of the former mill across Crabtree Creek, and an interpretive plaque describing the site. The mill itself is not a restored building open for tours. View the remains from established public areas and do not treat private property, the dam structure, or the opposite bank as an invitation to enter.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
Visit Raleigh and Yates Mill Associates ↗
Can I fish at Lassiter Mill Park? +

Fishing takes place along Crabtree Creek where signs and conditions permit. Carry a current North Carolina fishing license and follow posted water and harvest rules. Banks and rocks near the dam can be steep, slippery, flooded, or unstable; choose an established safe position and never enter a closed construction area.
