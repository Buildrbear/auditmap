What people say

Roanoke Park is a compact 1.6-acre Five Points neighborhood park with mature shade, woodchip and sand play areas for young and older children, a long boardwalk, open lawn, picnic shelter, concrete basketball court, and unlit grass volleyball court.

Where should I park for Roanoke Park? +

Route to 1500 Cherokee Drive and use legal neighborhood street parking where signs allow. The City does not list a dedicated park lot. Do not block driveways, intersections, or narrow residential circulation, and avoid relying on nearby private property. Arrive early for popular toddler meetups because curb space is limited.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Which side of Roanoke Park should I enter from? +

Use the Cherokee Drive address for navigation. The narrow 1.6-acre park has play and court areas near one end and a long boardwalk connecting toward the picnic shelter and lawn. Because neighborhood streets border the park, supervise children closely while arriving and use the boardwalk rather than cutting through planted edges.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Are there restrooms at Roanoke Park? +

No. Raleigh's official playground directory lists no restroom at Roanoke Park, and there is no community center on the 1.6-acre site. Plan a shorter visit with young children and do not assume nearby homes or businesses provide public facilities.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
What time does Roanoke Park close? +

Roanoke Park is open from dawn to dusk daily under Raleigh's general park rule. The basketball court and grass volleyball court are unlit, and the shelter has no electricity, so the park is not designed for nighttime use.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Is Roanoke Park free? +

Yes. General use of the playgrounds, boardwalk, lawn, shelter, basketball court, and grass volleyball court is free and first come. The City lists no reservable facility or admission charge on the park page; existing users should share the small site and shelter courteously.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Is Roanoke Park wheelchair and stroller accessible? +

A long boardwalk connects the picnic-shelter side with a sitting area by the playground, but Raleigh does not publish accessible parking, grade, transfer, or play-component details for this park. Woodchips, sand, grass, curb approaches, and neighborhood street parking can be barriers; contact Raleigh Parks for route-specific help.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
What ages is Roanoke Park best for? +

The City lists separate woodchip play areas for ages 2-5 and 5-12. Raleigh's citywide playground inventory also lists tot swings and sand at Roanoke Park and notes that there is no restroom. The play areas are not enclosed, so close supervision matters.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 4, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
What sports can I play at Roanoke Park? +

The park has one concrete outdoor basketball court and one unlit grass volleyball court. Both are typically first come, first served. Bring your own ball and volleyball equipment, and do not expect court lighting, a staffed equipment desk, locker rooms, or restrooms.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Does Roanoke Park have a picnic shelter? +

Yes. The small first-come shelter has one table, one grill, and no electricity. A boardwalk connects it to the sitting and playground area. There is no restroom, and the shelter cannot be assumed available for a private party, so keep groups proportionate to the compact neighborhood park.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 3, 2026
City of Raleigh ↗
Is Roanoke Park shaded? +

Roanoke Park's natural setting includes mature trees around the play area, so portions of the playground and connecting boardwalk receive tree shade. Shade changes by time of day and season, and trees are not safe lightning or high-wind shelter. The picnic shelter provides additional sun cover but has no electricity.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 4, 2026
