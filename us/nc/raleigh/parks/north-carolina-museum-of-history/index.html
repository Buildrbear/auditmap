What people say

North Carolina's flagship state history museum is fully closed during a $180 million expansion and infrastructure renovation, with a current fall 2028 reopening target. Its collection, digital tours, statewide education, traveling exhibits, offsite events, and relocated Museum Shop remain active.

Where should I park for the NC Museum of History? +

Do not park downtown for a normal museum visit: the entire 5 East Edenton Street building is closed during renovation and has no public galleries or visitor entrance. For an offsite museum event, use the parking instructions published for that event's host venue. For the separate Museum Shop at 108 Glenwood Avenue, check current downtown street, lot, and deck rules before traveling.

Can I enter the NC Museum of History building? +

No. The full downtown building, including all former galleries and visitor services, is closed to the public. Construction began in 2025. The project will relocate the future public entrance to the north side of Bicentennial Plaza to improve accessibility, but that entrance will not serve visitors until the museum reopens.

Are public restrooms available at the closed Museum of History? +

No visitor restrooms are available because the entire museum building is closed. Nearby state buildings, museums, restaurants, and event venues have separate entry rules and hours; do not assume they provide public restroom access. For an offsite museum program, confirm facilities with the named host venue.

What hours is the NC Museum of History open? +

The downtown museum has no public hours while the entire building is closed for renovation. The current official target is a fall 2028 reopening. Online resources remain available, while offsite festivals, virtual talks, traveling exhibitions, the Glenwood Avenue Museum Shop, and regional history museums each publish separate schedules.

Is the NC Museum of History free during renovation? +

There is no onsite admission because no public museum visit is available. Many digital tours and online resources can be viewed without museum admission. Offsite events, traveling-exhibit host venues, regional museums, workshops, and the Museum Shop may set their own prices or registration requirements; check the specific listing before attending.

Is the NC Museum of History accessible during renovation? +

The downtown building is not accessible to any public visitors because it is fully closed. The renovation specifically includes a relocated north-side entrance intended to improve access, plus modernized visitor and building systems. For current offsite or virtual programming, request accommodations from the listed organizer or host venue before registering.

When will the NC Museum of History reopen? +

The current official schedule calls for reopening in fall 2028. Major projects can change, so treat that as a target rather than a guaranteed opening day. Follow the museum's renovation FAQ, newsletter, and official social accounts for the date, preview events, new entrance instructions, gallery availability, and ticket information as reopening approaches.

What is changing at the NC Museum of History? +

The project adds about 40,000 square feet and roughly 25 percent more gallery space. Work includes new climate-control systems, leak repairs, a freight elevator, classrooms, a restaurant, community and office spaces, and a relocated accessible north-side entrance. The state authorized $180 million for the renovation and modernization.

What happened to the Museum of History artifacts? +

The museum says its collection of more than 150,000 artifacts was moved to secure, environmentally appropriate storage during construction. The institution continues collections care, interpretation, education, and statewide outreach while the public building is closed. Artifacts appearing in offsite exhibitions represent selected loans, not an open storage facility.

Can I visit the NC Museum of History online? +

Yes. Official digital experiences include a 360-degree Story of North Carolina tour, a 1920s Drugstore tour, and topic tours on aviation, World War I, women, American Indians, African American life and culture, and object-based stories. Some Exhibit+ tours request a short form before viewing so the museum can measure statewide reach.

Where can I see Museum of History exhibits during closure? +

The museum continues traveling exhibitions and partnerships at host venues. Current traveling topics include North Carolina vacation history, Vietnam veteran photography, Margaret Morley's mountain photography, the First Gulf War, Lewis Hine's child-labor photographs, and North Carolina barbecue. Locations and dates vary, so use the museum event and exhibit pages rather than going to Edenton Street.

Is the NC Museum of History shop open? +

The museum FAQ directs visitors to a separate Museum Shop storefront at 108 Glenwood Avenue in Raleigh. It is not inside the closed Edenton Street building. Verify the shop's current hours, accessibility, parking, and holiday schedule before traveling; shop availability does not mean museum galleries are open.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 4, 2026

What history museum can I visit while the Raleigh museum is closed? +

North Carolina's seven regional state history museums remain open across the state, including the Museum of the Albemarle, Museum of the Cape Fear, Mountain Gateway Museum, Tobacco Farm Life Museum, Graveyard of the Atlantic Museum, and maritime museums in Beaufort and Southport. In Raleigh, the City of Raleigh Museum, Pope House Museum, Mordecai Historic Park, State Capitol, and Executive Mansion offer separate local-history experiences with their own schedules.
