Where should I park for the NC Museum of Natural Sciences? +

Green Square parking deck is the most direct option for the Nature Research Center: enter from Edenton Street between Salisbury and McDowell, then leave the deck's north side at the NRC rear entrance. An above-ground paid lot is one block east with Jones and Edenton entrances. Downtown state-government parking is currently $2 per hour; limited metered spaces line Jones and Edenton streets, and weekend parking is generally easier.

Which entrance should I use at the NC Museum of Natural Sciences? +

Use 11 West Jones Street at Bicentennial Plaza for the Nature Exploration Center. The Nature Research Center has a front entrance at 121 West Jones Street and a rear entrance at 120 West Edenton Street beside Green Square deck. All three are valid public entrances; choose by parking and the exhibits you want first.

Where are accessible and family restrooms in the museum? +

Accessible stalls are in public restrooms on every floor of both buildings. Single-use restrooms are on the first floor of each building. Baby-changing stations are in all NEC restrooms and selected NRC restrooms. A combined quiet space, nursing room, and fixed-height universal changing table is on the NEC first floor behind Coastal Hall, with a sink and toilet across the hall.

What hours is the NC Museum of Natural Sciences open? +

Beginning May 25, 2026, the downtown museum is open Monday-Sunday from 10 a.m.-5 p.m.; last admission is 4:45 p.m. The published 2026 closures remaining after summer are Thanksgiving Day, December 24, and December 25. Cafes, shops, labs, theaters, special exhibits, and daily programs can have shorter schedules.

Is the NC Museum of Natural Sciences free? +

Yes. General admission and permanent exhibits at the downtown museum are free, and donations are appreciated. The WRAL 3D Theater, traveling or special exhibitions, and selected programs can require paid tickets. Check the day's calendar and ticket desk rather than assuming every timed experience is included.

Is the NC Museum of Natural Sciences wheelchair accessible? +

Both downtown buildings have automatic public entrance doors, elevators to every level, accessible restrooms on every floor, accessible theater seating, and free manual transport wheelchairs at Information Desks. The indoor second-floor bridge has automatic doors; the outdoor third-floor bridge does not. The museum does not supply powered chairs or scooters.

What is the difference between the Nature Exploration and Research Centers? +

The east-side Nature Exploration Center at 11 West Jones Street has four floors of habitat halls, live animals, aquariums, geology, North Carolina nature, dinosaurs, and the WRAL 3D Theater. The west-side three-floor Nature Research Center at 121 West Jones emphasizes working laboratories, current science, biodiversity, health, the Daily Planet Theater, Dueling Dinosaurs, and interactive research experiences.

Where are the dinosaurs at the NC Museum of Natural Sciences? +

Dinosaurs are on the third floor of both downtown buildings and the first floor of the Nature Research Center. Dueling Dinosaurs is in the NRC and may involve specific exhibit or program availability. Start at the NRC rear entrance from Green Square for the shortest approach to its first-floor dinosaur content, or follow floor maps and indoor bridges for a full visit.

How do the museum's two buildings connect? +

An indoor bridge connects the buildings on the second floor and has automatic doors. A second bridge connects the third floors outdoors, but its doors are not automatic. Visitors using mobility devices or avoiding weather should use the second-floor bridge and elevators rather than relying on the third-floor route.

What sensory supports does the museum offer? +

Free noise-canceling headphones are available at both Information Desks. The NEC first-floor quiet space can support sensory breaks, and the museum publishes a sensory map and bilingual social stories. Assistive listening devices can be reserved for presentations, movie transcripts are available at the WRAL box office, and some films offer open-caption devices.

Can we eat at the NC Museum of Natural Sciences? +

Yes. Daily Planet Cafe in the Nature Research Center currently operates Monday-Friday 8 a.m.-4 p.m., Saturday 9 a.m.-4 p.m., and Sunday 10 a.m.-4 p.m., with indoor and patio seating. Acro Cafe in the Nature Exploration Center is listed Tuesday-Sunday 11 a.m.-4 p.m. Cafe schedules can change independently of museum hours.

Are strollers available at the museum? +

Yes. The museum's access guide says strollers are available free at both Information Desks, subject to availability. Elevators operate in each building, and the indoor second-floor bridge offers the most weather-protected building connection. Ask staff for the easiest route around crowded halls, theaters, labs, and special exhibits.

Are 3D movies included with museum admission? +

No. Permanent exhibits are free, but WRAL 3D Theater movies require a separate ticket. Check the day's title, showtime, age fit, format, runtime, captioning, and availability. Accessible seating is provided; printed transcripts are available, and open-caption devices can be requested for some films.

Is the NC Museum of Natural Sciences closed for construction? +

The downtown Jones Street museum is open daily under the current schedule. The construction closure applies to Prairie Ridge Ecostation at 1671 Gold Star Drive, a separate 45-acre West Raleigh site closed to general visitation beginning April 1, 2026, with reopening anticipated in 2027. Only registered Prairie Ridge programs continue during construction.

Can I take transit to the museum? +

Yes. The downtown campus is in the State Government Complex along Jones Street and is reachable by downtown bus routes and walking connections. Bus loading and unloading is available on Jones Street near the Nature Exploration Center entrance. Check GoRaleigh routes and current State Government Complex street or event closures before traveling.
