What people say

The WNC Nature Center is a 42-acre wildlife park focused on Southern Appalachian species, with outdoor habitat trails and more than 60 species including black bears, cougars, wolves, otters, red pandas, reptiles, amphibians, and birds, plus daily activities and education programs.

Areas may still be affected by Helene. Check current Asheville recovery conditions before visiting. To help Western North Carolina recover, donate to the NC Community Foundation Disaster Relief Fund.

What address or entrance should I use for Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

75 Gashes Creek Road, Asheville, NC 28805. Because the former bridge route was lost to Helene, approach via Azalea Road, continue to the roundabout, cross the signal-controlled one-way metal bridge, then turn left onto Gashes Creek Road.

Where should I park for Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Use the on-site lots after the temporary metal-bridge approach. On busy days follow signs to overflow parking at John B. Lewis Soccer Complex; the ticket office is cashless.

Is Western North Carolina Nature Center free? +

Current admission is $16.95 adults and youth 13-15, $15.95 seniors 65+, $12.95 children 3-12, and free for ages two and under, plus tax. Asheville residents receive lower rates, and active-duty or retired military enter free with ID.

Are there restrooms at Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Visitor restrooms are available within the developed Nature Center during operating hours. Use the entrance-area facilities before longer outdoor habitat loops when needed.

What accessibility information should I know about Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

The center publishes dedicated accessibility guidance, but its outdoor campus includes hills and varied distances. Review the current accessibility page or contact staff for mobility, sensory, service-animal, stroller, or restroom needs before arrival.

What are the paths or trails like at Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Outdoor walking routes connect animal habitats across the 42-acre hillside campus. Expect at least a mile for many visits, with grades and weather exposure; special behind-the-scenes programs can explicitly require a mile of walking and steep hills.

What can families do at Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Children can see native and regional wildlife, attend daily activities, and learn about habitats and animal care. Guests under 16 must be accompanied by an adult, and children must be supervised at all times.

Can I bring a dog to Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Pets are not permitted inside the wildlife center. Contact the center in advance about service-animal procedures because proximity to animal habitats may require specific routing or accommodations.

Can I picnic at Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Use only designated visitor areas and follow posted food rules; do not feed animals or bring food into restricted habitat zones. Group visits and programs require advance planning.

What landmarks or areas should I look for at Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Popular habitats feature black bears, cougars, red and gray wolves, river otters, red pandas, farm animals, reptiles, amphibians, and native birds, with mountain landscaping and educational exhibits throughout the route.

What closures or conditions can affect Western North Carolina Nature Center? +

Animal availability, severe weather, habitat work, special events, and the temporary post-Helene access route can affect a visit. Tickets are not refunded or rain-checked solely for animal availability or weather, so check conditions first.
