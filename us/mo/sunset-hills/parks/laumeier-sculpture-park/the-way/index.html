destination

The Way

12580 Rott Road, St. Louis, MO 63127, Sunset Hills
Hours ⌄
Published schedule Open daily 7:00 a.m. until 30 minutes after sunset, year-round, except selected major events. Aronson Fine Arts Center and galleries keep shorter Thursday-Sunday seasonal hours and may close for private events.
Navigate

What people say

Alexander Liberman's The Way is a monumental red steel composition on the main lawn and one of Laumeier's defining landmarks; do not climb it. The entire park closes to ordinary visitors during the Thursday-Sunday Art Fair weekend and selected After Dark programming. Check event and gallery notices, and never climb an artwork unless its label specifically permits interaction.

Where should I park for The Way? +

Free parking is available at the lower Rott Road entrance and west entrance. Art Fair and major-event parking uses special off-site or controlled arrangements, so follow event instructions rather than ordinary arrival directions.

Is The Way free? +

Park grounds, permanent collection and ordinary gallery access are free. Classes, special programs, food and major events such as the Art Fair may charge or require registration.

How accessible is The Way? +

Aronson and the education lab are step-free. The paved Central Pathway follows steep natural grades that exceed ADA standards; wheelchairs are available under current checkout procedures and tactile bronze artwork models support visitors with low vision.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 6, 2026
St. Louis County Parks and Laumeier Sculpture Park ↗
Are dogs allowed at The Way? +

Leashed dogs are welcome in outdoor galleries but may be prohibited during the Art Fair, Laumeier After Dark and other events. Service animals may enter public areas.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 6, 2026

Is The Way good for children? +

Large-scale sculpture, open lawns, short trails, family programs and the Children's Sculpture Garden support exploration, but most artworks must not be touched or climbed unless an object label explicitly allows it.
