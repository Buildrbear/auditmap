Are there restrooms near Nomade? +

Do not plan on a dedicated public restroom inside the open-air sculpture park; the official visitor page does not list one. The adjacent Central Library and nearby businesses have their own hours and access rules, so use a staffed downtown facility before entering the park if restroom access is important.

How accessible is Nomade? +

Yes. The Art Center describes it as an accessible, pedestrian-friendly setting. Street crossings, lawn conditions, temporary events, and winter weather can still affect the easiest route between individual works.

Are dogs allowed at Nomade? +

Leashed dogs may accompany visitors in the outdoor city sculpture park, but it is not an off-leash area. Keep pets away from every artwork, pedestal, planting bed, and food setup, remove waste at the perimeter receptacles, and never allow climbing or contact with a sculpture. The separate Des Moines Art Center museum permits only service dogs indoors. Separately operated buildings, beaches, gardens, and attractions can set stricter rules.

Is Nomade useful for a family visit? +

Nomade is Jaume Plensa's monumental seated figure built from white steel letters and one of Pappajohn Sculpture Park's most recognizable works. Families are welcome, and the Art Center provides a printable exploration guide with movement, observation, and discussion activities for all ages. This is an art park rather than a playground: children must not climb, sit on, or touch sculptures except Scott Burton's Seating for Eight.
