What people say

Urban Trees Interpretive Marker is a mapped history landmark at Jessie Ball duPont Park; navigate to this point, read the on-site context, and treat memorial activity respectfully. The Treaty Oak name comes from a 1930s newspaper story rather than a documented treaty. The genuine significance is the approximately 250-year-old live oak and the long preservation effort surrounding it.

A small amount of car parking is listed, but the Southbank setting and nearby development can make access tight. Read every curb and lot sign and consider walking from a legal Southbank space.

Does it cost money to visit Urban Trees Interpretive Marker? +

Ordinary park, picnic-table, lawn, and Treaty Oak viewing access is free. Nearby parking, food, events, and private attractions cost separately.

How accessible is Urban Trees Interpretive Marker? +

Developed entrances and paths provide access toward the tree and picnic area. Roots, low limbs, lawn, construction, and tree-protection barriers can limit the closest approach.

Sourced answer Researched Aug 8, 2026

Are dogs allowed at Urban Trees Interpretive Marker? +

Keep dogs leashed and under close control unless current signs explicitly provide otherwise. Protect the tree's root zone and keep pets away from picnics, low branches, and restoration areas.

Is Urban Trees Interpretive Marker good for children? +

The enormous low limbs and shaded lawn create a memorable short stop, but this is not a playground. Children must not climb, hang from, carve, or damage the historic tree.
