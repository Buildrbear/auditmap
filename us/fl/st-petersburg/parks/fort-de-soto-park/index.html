What people say

Fort De Soto is Pinellas County's 1,136-acre island park with nearly three miles of beach, a historic fort, campground, fishing piers, boat ramp, paved recreation trail, nature and canoe trails, playgrounds, picnic shelters, accessible nature trail, and designated dog beach.

What hours is Fort De Soto Park open? +

Fort De Soto is open seven days a week from 7 a.m. to sunset. Fishing piers use the same published hours but may close separately for cleaning, repairs, weather, or storm damage.

How do I pay for parking at Fort De Soto? +

Daily parking costs $6 per vehicle. Pay with an onsite terminal, supported parking app, pay-by-text, or an annual pass; app and text payments add a $0.35 convenience fee, while the park no longer accepts cash at an entry booth.

What address should I use for Fort De Soto Park? +

Use 3500 Pinellas Bayway South, Tierra Verde, FL 33715. Once inside the large five-island park, follow the official map to North Beach, East Beach, the historic fort, dog beach, boat ramp, campground, or fishing pier.

What trails are at Fort De Soto? +

A wide paved recreation trail connects beaches, the campground, boat ramp, and historic fort. The park also has short natural-surface trails, a canoe trail, and a 2,200-foot barrier-free interpretive nature trail.

Is there an accessible nature trail at Fort De Soto? +

Yes. The 2,200-foot Barrier-Free Nature Trail is a self-guided interpretive route designed to make the park's natural setting available to visitors across mobility levels. The official map also marks accessible facilities.

Where can dogs go on the beach at Fort De Soto? +

Dogs are allowed on the designated dog beach and in separate fenced areas for large and small dogs with water stations. Outside designated leash-free areas, pets must follow County leash and park rules; other County beaches generally prohibit dogs.

What parts of Fort De Soto are closed? +

Storm damage continues to close the East Loop and portions of the seawall near the historic site. Boat-ramp access is limited to one public floating dock, North Beach restroom repairs and other projects remain active, and permitted races can affect roads. Check the County notice immediately before visiting.

Where are restrooms at Fort De Soto Park? +

Restrooms are distributed near the major beaches, picnic areas, campground, boat ramp, and other developed facilities; they are not available at every remote shoreline. Use the official park diagram for locations, and check repair notices because hurricane work can temporarily affect individual buildings.

Are there playgrounds at Fort De Soto Park? +

Yes. Fort De Soto lists playgrounds among its family amenities, with locations shown on the official park map. Choose your beach or picnic destination first and route to the nearest marked playground rather than expecting one at every beach access.

Can I reserve a picnic shelter at Fort De Soto? +

Yes. The park has 15 reservable picnic shelters. Storm repairs and burn restrictions can change table or grill availability, so review the current County notice and reservation listing before planning food preparation or a large gathering.

Where can I find shade or weather shelter at Fort De Soto? +

Fort De Soto has 15 reservable picnic shelters, plus tree shade along selected nature and picnic areas. Beaches, fishing piers, seawalls, boat areas, paved trail, historic fort, and much of the campground are exposed to sun, wind, and storm surge. Concessions and the Quartermaster Museum follow separate hours. Picnic shelters are not hurricane, tornado, lightning, or evacuation shelters; obey Pinellas County closure and evacuation orders and leave the barrier islands well before dangerous weather arrives.

Can I take public transit to Fort De Soto Park? +

No regular PSTA fixed-route bus enters Fort De Soto Park. The park is isolated at the end of the Tierra Verde causeway and its beaches, fort, piers, campground, dog beach, and trail are spread across many miles. Plan a car, taxi, rideshare, bicycle trip, or arranged pickup, confirm the return before arrival, and do not expect to walk safely from a distant bus stop while carrying beach or camping gear.

Where can visitors swim at Fort De Soto Park? +

Fort De Soto has beach swimming, not a splash pad or pool. Swim only from a designated beach area and check posted signs for seasonal lifeguard availability, water conditions, rip-current risk, and temporary restrictions. North Beach and East Beach have the park's two large swim centers, with concessions at North Beach. County rules prohibit portable pools, water slides, and water guns; pets may enter the water only at the separately designated dog beach.

Where is the historic fort at Fort De Soto Park? +

The historic district is on Mullet Key and centers on Battery Laidley, the Quartermaster Museum, and a self-guided trail marking former military buildings. Battery Laidley contains the last four surviving carriage-mounted 12-inch seacoast mortars in the continental United States. The museum is free and currently listed open daily from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Route to the historic fort rather than North Beach, East Beach, campground, or dog beach, which are separate destinations across the island park.

Sourced answer Researched Jul 31, 2026

How much does Fort De Soto Park cost? +

Daily parking is $6 per vehicle; mobile-app or pay-by-text transactions add a $0.35 convenience fee. Camping, boat launching, ferries, rentals, and reservations cost extra.
