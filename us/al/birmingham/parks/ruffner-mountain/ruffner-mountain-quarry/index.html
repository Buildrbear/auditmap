What people say

Ruffner Mountain Quarry is the exposed limestone destination reached from the preserve's Quarry Trail network, where visitors can see rock faces and look for fossil evidence of ancient sea life. This is a trail destination, not a drive-up overlook. Stay on marked trails, keep back from quarry and cliff edges, carry out all trash, and allow enough time to return before the preserve gate closes. Restrooms, water, and staff assistance are concentrated near the Nature Center and pavilion rather than at the quarry.

What fees apply at Ruffner Mountain Quarry? +

The preserve does not charge admission. Non-members arriving by vehicle must purchase a $5 daily visitor parking permit; Ruffner members park with their current permit.

How accessible is Ruffner Mountain Quarry? +

The Nature Center and pavilion are the most developed visitor facilities. Preserve trails are natural-surface routes with roots, rocks, grades, quarry terrain, and weather-related mud, so review the official map and contact staff for a route matched to specific mobility needs.

Are dogs allowed at Ruffner Mountain Quarry? +

Yes. Ruffner Mountain welcomes dogs on its trails, but they must remain leashed. Leashes protect pets from wildlife, cliffs, quarry hazards, other dogs, and sensitive habitat, and owners must carry out waste. Separately operated buildings, beaches, gardens, and attractions can set stricter rules.

Is Ruffner Mountain Quarry useful for a family visit? +

Ruffner Mountain Quarry is the exposed limestone destination reached from the preserve's Quarry Trail network, where visitors can see rock faces and look for fossil evidence of ancient sea life. Jefferson County Greenways does not list conventional playground equipment at Ruffner Mountain. Family visits center on the Nature Center's Wildlife Ambassadors, seasonal encounters and programs, pollinator gardens, the pavilion, and age-appropriate forest trails. Choose another Birmingham park for swings or slides. At Ruffner, keep children on marked routes and away from closed mine openings, quarry edges, wildlife, and rough terrain; bicycles are not permitted on the trails.
